# Episode Nineteen: Four in the Morning

## Arc
Episode Eighteen ended with Marco sent up the coast to Aldo's cousin's bakery, four in the morning, flour in his nice shoes. Three weeks later the bakery's van brings bread into Port Paloma before dawn, and Marco is driving it.
In every bread basket on Nico's streets there are sesame rolls with betting slips in the bag: a numbers book run out of a bread van, collected by the van's mate, Dario Venn. Gina finds one in Benny's lunch.
Dr. Vos helps Nico choose between proof for Aldo's table (respect) and simply taking the van (deal). Dario runs, and talks: it was Marco's own idea, to earn his way back to his uncle. The table, and then Gina, decide what the bread is now.

## Story reasons
- The stake-out and tail: a man sent away to bake does not drive back into the town he was sent from unless he wants something in it. Nico has to see where the van goes after the bread, quietly, because Aldo's nephew is Aldo's business until Nico can prove otherwise.
- The rounds: the slip turned up in his own son's lunch. The quickest way to know how far the book reaches is to ask the men who buy the bread, and Gina is the one who sends him.
- The therapy choice: Nico showed Aldo respect last episode and got his streets taken for it. The session is where he decides whether respect still means asking the uncle first, or whether business means taking what is his.
- The Back of the Van: proof is the book itself, taken past the lookout the way Marco once took Nico's things; taking the van is the same act done loudly. Either way the bread is driven carefully, because it is somebody's breakfast.
- Rolling Pin: a book has a collector, and collectors come on Fridays. A chase, not a beating, because Nico needs him talking: the twist (Marco alone, not Aldo) is what keeps a rival crew from becoming a war.
- Four in the Morning: the episode's question (family vs business) is answered twice: by Aldo at the table about his nephew, and by Gina at the kitchen table about her son.
