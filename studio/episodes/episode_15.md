# Episode Fifteen: The Button

## Arc
Episode Fourteen ended with the wire left in the Pelican telephone, and Enzo told to buy Petey a sandwich instead of finding a rat to earn his button.
Uncle Sergio says the books are open for one name this year. Enzo is sure it is his; Aldo Ferro puts up his sister's boy, Marco Pellegrini, who is already collecting on Nico's streets.
Nico decides whether the boy is ready, takes back the Haskell load Marco stole, and wins the name at a sit-down. Then Gina asks whether he wanted the boy to become him.

## Story reasons
- The rounds: Nico cannot hit Aldo's nephew on hearsay. Asking the people who pay him is how he finds out it is real, and that Marco is collecting so people will see him, not for the money.
- The fight and chase: Marco has to be put down in front of Enzo, but not hurt badly, because there is a sit-down coming. Fists only is the lesson: Nico stops Enzo from finishing it.
- The therapy choice: the episode's question is whether the boy is ready. Last week he nearly hurt Petey to earn something. Taking him along or letting him go alone is Nico's answer to that question, and it changes the next night.
- The tail or the ram: with Nico beside him, Enzo learns to hang back. Alone, he is seen, and Nico has to clean it up. The same mistake as Petey's in Episode Fourteen, but this time Enzo is the one being chased.
- The stake-out and photograph: Aldo will deny everything at the table, so Nico needs proof. A picture of Marco beside a stolen Haskell pickup is that proof.
- The sneak, the shake and the careful drive: Haskell has paid Nico since Episode Two. Taking the load back quietly and whole is the point. A smashed load or a shooting would be Marco's way of doing it.
- The sit-down: being there first, respect and a deal are how a boss wins without a war. The choices show whether Nico can do what he is teaching the boy.
- Gina: the wife's doubt is the cost of the episode. Nico got the boy the thing he wanted, and she asks whether that thing is any good for him.
