# Episode Twenty: Sleepy Biscotti

## Arc
Episode Nineteen ended with Benny's forty dollars from a horse called Sleepy Biscotti, and a nephew sent up the coast for running a book of his own. Two weeks later Mrs. Albright rings: there is a book at Our Lady of the Bay, lunch money on horses, and Benny is the bookie.
A tout called Hollis Grebe hands the boy tip sheets by the pond, and keeps the kids' stakes in a biscuit tin in his green station wagon, for Dusty Kilbane, the small-time rival from the park.
Dr. Vos helps Nico choose between talking to his son first (calm) and going straight at the tout (deal). Nico takes the tin back from the motor lodge, the Pelican table settles Kilbane, and Gina decides how Benny gives the money back.

## Story reasons
- Lunch Money: the principal's call is the family half of family vs business arriving at Nico's door. He follows instead of grabbing the boy because he has to know who the man in the wagon is before Benny knows he is known.
- The Form Guide: a father cannot ask his son what he is doing without the son lying; he can listen. The photograph is how Augie names the tout and his boss.
- Like Father: Benny copied Marco's scheme from Nineteen in miniature, and copied Nico. The session is where Nico decides whether this is a family matter (talk to the boy) or business (deal with the man).
- Before the Bell: the calm road puts father and son in a car with a clock running, the only place Nico ever talks to Benny; the deal road is a chase because a tout who runs is a tout who talks.
- The Biscuit Tin: the money is the kids'. Taking it back quietly, before Saturday's race, is the only way the children get their lunches and Kilbane gets nothing to bargain with.
- Sleepy Biscotti: the table settles the rival (business); the kitchen settles the son (family). Gina has the last word, as she did on the forty dollars.
